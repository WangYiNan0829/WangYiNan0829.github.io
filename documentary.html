01 / The Factory

Before a shoe leaves the line,
it passes through many hands.

Photographed in August 2026, this project follows several days inside a shoe factory. I documented the labor along its production lines and spoke with two women at very different stages of their working lives.

The roar of machinery, stifling heat, and endlessly repeated movements shape the rhythm of each day. Yet beyond the shared label of “worker,” each person has a different reason for staying.

“Whatever others may say,
I earn my money through my own hard work.”

— Xiaodan Ren

02 / Their Stories

One production line.
Two stages of life.

01

The Newcomer

Xiaodan Ren

In her early twenties, Xiaodan left high school and entered the workforce. She had been at the factory for about a month when we met. She speaks openly about regretting her education, while seeing this job as a way to save toward a different life.

“I want to work for a few years, save some money, then return home and open a small shop of my own.”
02

The Working Mother

Jing Tang

In her thirties, Jing has worked in the shoe industry for more than a decade. She moves constantly between the factory and home. Her income supports her child’s education, while time together remains the part of life she can never quite recover.

“Going back and forth between the factory and home can leave me overwhelmed, but I can’t simply stop taking care of my child.”

03 / Building Trust

Trust takes time.

The first interview did not go as planned. Factory noise drowned out every voice, the equipment and camera positions were not fully prepared, and the unfamiliarity between us brought the conversation to an end in under twenty minutes.

Over the following days, I kept returning—photographing work and lunch breaks, waiting for shifts to end, and speaking with the women away from the camera. Only when we were no longer simply “photographer” and “subject” did their real lives begin to enter the frame.

04 / Reflection

“Hard work” was no longer an abstract idea.

After several days on the factory floor, I began to understand how the heat, noise, repetition, and exhaustion of long hours make up the workers’ everyday lives.

Speaking with them also showed me that people remain in this industry for different reasons: some for survival, some for family, and some in the hope that a few years of effort might lead to a new beginning.

I began by trying to document their work. In the end, I wanted to record their lives as ordinary people.
